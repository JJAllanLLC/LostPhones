What to Do If You Lost Your iPhone – Immediate Checklist

Published January 2025 · Updated October 6, 2026

By JJ Allan

Take a deep breath. You're in the right place.

Losing your iPhone is stressful, but most people get their phone back or protect their data when they follow the right steps quickly. This checklist is the same one I used when I lost mine — and it worked.

Quick Printable Checklist (Do These First)

  • ☐ 1. Check Find My at icloud.com/find
  • ☐ 2. Play Sound (even if on silent)
  • ☐ 3. Mark it as Lost promptly (add contact details only if it appears misplaced, not stolen)
  • ☐ 4. Review Apple Account details; change the password if Find My was off or account details changed
  • ☐ 5. Call your carrier to suspend service and ask about IMEI blocking on participating networks
  • ☐ 6. Change email, banking, and payment app passwords
  • ☐ 7. Add Port Freeze / Number Lock with carrier
  • ☐ 8. Watch for scams — never trust "found your phone" texts
  • ☐ 9. If not found, erase remotely (last resort)
  • ☐ 10. Enable 2FA everywhere and bookmark this guide

(Print this page or screenshot it — it's designed to be your one-page emergency sheet.)

Full Step-by-Step Guide

1. Check Find My and mark the iPhone as lost

Open Apple Find Devices, select the missing iPhone, review its available location, and turn on Mark as Lost. If it is offline, the command can take effect when the phone reconnects. Use the dead-battery guide for the limits and next steps.

2. Protect the phone number if the iPhone stays missing

Use your carrier's official app or website to suspend the line or ask about SIM protection. This is especially important after theft, when the phone cannot be located, or when you cannot receive account-verification codes.

3. Review accounts based on actual risk

Review your Apple Account for changes you did not make. Change passwords when you see unauthorized access or believe a password is known; changing every password is not automatically required. Review banks and payment apps promptly if the unlocked phone or financial accounts may be exposed.

4. Document theft or loss when it helps

If the iPhone was stolen, or an insurer or carrier requires documentation, use the appropriate official local police-reporting channel and keep the report number in your own records. Do not risk confronting someone to retrieve the phone.

5. Treat erase as a last resort

Consider remote erase only after safer, reversible steps are exhausted and recovery looks unlikely. Erasing can stop later location updates.
